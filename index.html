The Roadster that leaves the ground.

Tesla's second-generation Roadster uses SpaceX-derived cold gas thrusters to hop and flip. Change the numbers, fire the thrusters, and let a Java 21 server do the physics.

Tesla has not published thruster specifications. These figures come from a textbook model of compressed air expanding through a nozzle, using the sliders above as assumptions.

How it works

1. Store gas

Air is compressed into a high-pressure tank. Pressure is stored energy, with no fire involved.

2. Open the valve

The gas rushes through a nozzle that turns pressure into speed, up to roughly 700 m/s.

3. Newton pushes back

Throwing gas down pushes the car up. Reports also describe a multi-mode suction system for grip and spin.

The physics

Thrust F = ṁ · vₑ Exhaust speed vₑ = √( 2γ/(γ−1) · R·T · [1 − (pₑ/p₀)^((γ−1)/γ)] ) Specific impulse Isp = vₑ / g₀ Rocket equation Δv = vₑ · ln( m₀ / m_f ) Hover time t = Isp · ln( m₀ / m_f )

To hover, thrust must equal weight, so a 1,800 kg car needs about 17.7 kN. Cold gas has a low exhaust speed, so it burns through gas fast. That is why the car only gets short hops, not a flight across town.

Rocket science

Combustion makes gas far hotter, so exhaust is faster and Isp is 4–5 times higher. Rockets also carry their oxidizer and work in space.

Market reality

Air taxis (eVTOL) are on the certification path with wings and electric rotors. Cold gas thrusters are a show-stopper, not a commuter.

Hurdles

Aviation rules, noise, safety, and energy cost. Musk himself said the car is not necessarily safety-optimized.
